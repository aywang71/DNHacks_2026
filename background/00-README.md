## Conventions used

- Text quoted from dnhacks.org is marked as verbatim. Everything else is research or clearly-labelled inference.
- Where a fact could not be verified, it says so rather than guessing. Known gaps: **Torus Systems** (partner, unidentifiable from public sources), **Jake Fischer's** exact title (as listed on the site, not independently confirmed), **F-ADA** (minimal public footprint).
- Two name collisions are flagged explicitly and should not be conflated: **Delve** (delvedc.com, DC research firm — the attending one) vs. delve.co (compliance startup); and **Andrew McCarthy** (White House anti-fraud CTO, ex-CISA — the attending one) vs. the National Review columnist.

## The three facts that most shape decisions

1. **The build window is 26 hours, not 36.** Hacking opens 10:00 a.m. Saturday; submission closes 12:00 p.m. Sunday, with three panels inside the Saturday afternoon block.
2. **The demo runs twice** — showcase at 1:00 p.m. to three separate judge groups, then finalist live demo at 4:00 p.m. Build it cache-first so it cannot fail on venue wifi three hours later.
3. **This is a buyer-and-operator audience, not an academic one.** Government CTOs, defense-tech founders, and investors. They reward a named user, a measured claim, an honest failure mode, and a credible path to deployment — over novelty.
